# A24 – Books and Reading

> [!abstract] Zadanie
> Talk about reading and its importance, the kinds of books and literary
> genres, your favourite book and author, and libraries and e-books.

- Fiction and non-fiction, and the kinds of book in each.
- The three main literary genres — poetry, prose and drama.
- Bookshops and libraries, and how borrowing works.
- Famous English-speaking writers.
- Your favourite book and why you like it.
- Printed books versus e-books and audiobooks.
- The crisis in reading — why people read less.

## 4. Modelová odpoveď

Reading is one of the oldest and, if you use a library, one of the cheapest ways of spending free time. Books are a source of information and entertainment at the same time; you can read them at home, on the bus, on the train or while waiting at the doctor's. Reading is fun when it is a hobby, though it can be rather less fun when you have to read something for school and there is a test the next day.

Literature is usually divided into two big categories. Non-fiction is written mainly to inform. It includes textbooks, which teach us a particular subject, dictionaries, which explain the meanings of words, encyclopaedias full of facts, biographies about the lives of famous people, autobiographies and memoirs, travel books, art books and cookery books. Fiction, on the other hand, is imaginative writing, and it comes in three main genres: poetry, prose and drama. Poetry includes epic and lyric poems, ballads and sonnets; prose includes novels, short stories and fairy tales; and drama includes tragedies and comedies written to be performed on stage.

Prose in particular has many popular sub-genres: science fiction, fantasy, thrillers, detective and crime stories, spy novels, historical novels, romances and westerns. When we analyse a book, we usually talk about the author, the narrator, the main characters, the plot, the setting, the conflict and the message.

We can buy books in a bookshop, where you can find originals, translations, simplified versions for language learners and, of course, bestsellers. Or we can borrow them from a library, which is free. In a library you need a lending card, you can keep a book for a certain lending period, you can usually extend it, and if you return it late you pay a small fine. Libraries also have study rooms where you can work in silence.

The English-speaking world has given us an extraordinary number of great writers. William Shakespeare wrote *Hamlet*, *Romeo and Juliet* and *Macbeth* and is probably the most famous dramatist in history. Charles Dickens described the poverty of Victorian England, Jane Austen wrote about society and marriage, and Arthur Conan Doyle created Sherlock Holmes. From America there is Mark Twain, who wrote *The Adventures of Tom Sawyer*, Ernest Hemingway and John Steinbeck, both Nobel Prize winners, and Edgar Allan Poe, the father of the detective story. Among modern authors, J. R. R. Tolkien and J. K. Rowling have probably done more than anybody to get young people reading again.

As for my own attitude, I do read, though probably less than I should. What I enjoy most is science fiction and books about technology, and I read a lot of technical material online in English. My favourite book is one I read a few years ago and have gone back to since; what I liked about it was that the story kept surprising me and that the characters felt like real people rather than types.

Finally there is the question of the printed book versus the e-book. An e-reader is light, you can carry hundreds of books on it, and the books are cheaper. Audiobooks let you „read" while travelling or cooking. But personally I still prefer a printed book — you can see how far you have got, you can lend it to a friend, and it does not run out of battery. People often say that reading is in crisis because books are expensive, nobody has time, and films and the internet are easier. There is something in that, but I think a good book still has no real competition.

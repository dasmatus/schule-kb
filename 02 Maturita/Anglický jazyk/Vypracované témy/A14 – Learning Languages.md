# A14 – Learning Languages

> [!abstract] Zadanie
> Talk about why people learn foreign languages, how languages are taught and
> learned, the world languages, and your own experience with English.

## 1. Osnova témy

- Why people learn foreign languages — necessity, work, travel, study, pleasure.
- What „mastering" a language actually means — the four skills.
- Learning languages at school in Slovakia, and which languages are taught.
- Formal and informal learning; bilingual and multilingual families.
- What makes learning easier or harder — motivation, age, personality, aptitude.
- English as a world language and its varieties.
- Your own experience with English and how you learn.
- Certificates, exams and the maturita.

## 2. Kľúčová slovná zásoba

- **kinds of language:** mother tongue (materinský jazyk), first language, second language, foreign language, dead language, official language, minority language, dialect
- **the four skills:** speaking, listening / listening comprehension, reading, writing; to master a language (ovládať jazyk), fluency (plynulosť), accuracy (presnosť), passive and active knowledge
- **levels:** total beginner, false beginner, pre-intermediate, intermediate, upper-intermediate, advanced; A1–C2 of the Common European Framework
- **learning settings:** formal setting (school, private lessons, language school), informal setting (at home, abroad), bilingual family, multilingual and multicultural environment, to acquire a language naturally
- **influential factors:** motivation (internal and external), attitude, aptitude (nadanie), age, personality — introverted vs. extroverted learners, linguistic intelligence, a supportive environment
- **classroom vocabulary:** vocabulary (slovná zásoba), grammar, pronunciation (výslovnosť), spelling, idiom, phrasal verb, false friend (falošný priateľ), textbook, workbook, dictionary, to revise (opakovať si), to translate, to look up a word
- **exams and certificates:** to take / sit an exam, to pass, to fail, oral and written part, listening comprehension, certificate, Cambridge exams (FCE, CAE), the maturita, school-leaving exam
- **varieties of English:** British English, American English, Australian English, Canadian English, Indian English, South African English
- **status of English:** lingua franca, the language of business, science and the internet, native speaker, non-native speaker
- **difficulties:** irregular verbs, tenses, articles, word order, pronunciation, spelling, shyness, fear of making mistakes, language barrier

## 4. Modelová odpoveď

People learn foreign languages for many different reasons. For some it is a hobby, for others it is a professional, educational or social necessity, and for a few it is a matter of prestige. But in this era of globalisation, speaking at least one foreign language has simply become a must. We want to communicate with the foreigners we meet when we travel, to understand songs and films, to read in the original, and to be able to work with people from other countries.

Mastering a foreign language means being able to do four things: speak it, write it, understand the spoken word and understand written texts. In practice many people manage only some of these skills. Somebody may speak English quite fluently but be unable to write it properly, and, surprisingly often, people understand a language well but are afraid to say anything in it. That fear of making mistakes is probably the biggest single obstacle in language learning.

Knowing a foreign language makes life easier in a very practical way. It helps you communicate abroad, make new friends and meet business partners. It makes travelling far more pleasant, because you can actually get to know a country and its culture instead of just looking at it. And it increases your self-confidence — there is a real satisfaction in watching a film without subtitles for the first time.

In Slovakia, formal learning starts at primary school and continues right up to university. English, German and Spanish are the most popular languages in our schools; French is also taught, and Russian, which almost disappeared after 1989, is gradually coming back. Many older Slovaks still have a passive knowledge of Russian. Some children start learning a foreign language very early, and children from bilingual families acquire two languages naturally, so both are effectively their mother tongues.

Several factors influence how well you learn. The most important is motivation, which can be internal, when you genuinely want to learn, or external, when somebody makes you. Age matters too: young children acquire pronunciation almost perfectly, while adults are usually better at grammar and can learn more systematically. Personality plays a role as well — extroverted learners are not afraid to speak and therefore practise more, while introverts often know more but say less. And of course the environment matters: nothing beats spending time in a country where the language is spoken.

English deserves special mention, because it has become the lingua franca of the modern world. It is the language of business, science, aviation, the internet and popular music. It also exists in many varieties — British, American, Australian, Canadian, Indian and others — which differ in pronunciation, vocabulary and sometimes spelling. Today most conversations in English take place between two non-native speakers, which is quite remarkable when you think about it.

As for me, I have been learning English since primary school, so it is about ten years now. What I find hardest are phrasal verbs and articles, because Slovak has nothing like them, while vocabulary comes to me fairly easily. Outside school I learn mostly by using the language: I watch series in English, I read technical documentation online, and I follow tutorials about programming, which are almost all in English. I am now preparing for the maturita, and after that I would like to take a certificate exam, because a recognised certificate is useful when you apply for a job.

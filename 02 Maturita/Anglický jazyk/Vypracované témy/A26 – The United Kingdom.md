# A26 – The United Kingdom

> [!abstract] Zadanie
> Talk about the general information on the United Kingdom, its location,
> population, industry and educational system, the interesting places in
> Britain, London as the capital city, famous British people, and customs and
> traditions.

## 1. Osnova témy

- General information — the four countries, location, population, currency.
- The political system — a constitutional monarchy.
- Climate, rivers and landscape.
- Industry and agriculture.
- The British system of education.
- Interesting places in England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.
- London — its history and its main sights.
- Famous British people, customs and traditions.

- **agriculture:** potatoes, sugar beet (cukrová repa), wheat (pšenica), sheep farming, dairy farming

## 4. Modelová odpoveď

The full name of the country is the United Kingdom of Great Britain and Northern Ireland, and it consists of four parts: England with its capital London, Scotland with Edinburgh, Wales with Cardiff and Northern Ireland with Belfast. It is worth being precise here, because Great Britain is the large island containing England, Scotland and Wales, while the United Kingdom also includes Northern Ireland. The country lies off the north-west coast of Europe, separated from the continent by the English Channel, and it has a population of roughly sixty-eight million. The currency is the pound sterling and the flag is called the Union Jack.

The UK is a constitutional monarchy and a parliamentary democracy. The monarch is the head of state but has mostly ceremonial powers, while real political power lies with the Prime Minister and Parliament. Parliament has two chambers, the House of Commons, whose members are elected, and the House of Lords. In 2016 the country voted to leave the European Union, and Brexit has shaped British politics ever since.

The climate is mild but changeable and rainy, which is probably why the weather is the national conversation topic. The most important river is the Thames, on which London stands, and the longest is the Severn. Scotland has mountains and hundreds of lakes, called lochs, while much of England is gentle green countryside.

Britain was the first industrialised country in the world. Today services — finance, banking, insurance and computing — are the most important sector, with the City of London one of the world's great financial centres. Traditional industries such as iron and steel, shipbuilding and textiles have declined. Agriculture produces potatoes, sugar beet, wheat, milk and meat, and sheep farming is typical of Wales and Scotland.

The education system differs from ours. Compulsory school attendance starts at five. Primary education is divided into infant and junior school, and at eleven children move to secondary school. Most pupils wear a school uniform, different for each school. At sixteen they take the GCSE examination, and those who want to continue study two more years and take their A-levels, which are needed for university. Oxford and Cambridge are the oldest and most prestigious universities.

There is a great deal to see. In England there is Stonehenge, a prehistoric stone circle whose purpose scientists still argue about; Stratford-upon-Avon, the birthplace of William Shakespeare; the university towns of Oxford and Cambridge; and Liverpool, the home of the Beatles. In Scotland there is Loch Ness with its famous monster, and the wild landscape of the Highlands.

London itself was founded by the Romans around 43 AD as Londinium, and its name comes from that. It survived the Great Fire in the seventeenth century and grew into the largest city in the world in the nineteenth. Today it is a multicultural city of some nine million people. The main sights are the Houses of Parliament with the clock tower housing Big Ben, Westminster Abbey where monarchs are crowned, Buckingham Palace with the Changing of the Guard, the Tower of London with the Crown Jewels, Tower Bridge, St Paul's Cathedral, Trafalgar Square and the British Museum.

As for famous Britons, there is Shakespeare, Newton, Darwin, Churchill, the Beatles and, more recently, writers like J. K. Rowling. And British customs are famous in themselves: afternoon tea, the pub, fish and chips, queuing patiently, driving on the left, and a dry sense of humour that takes some getting used to.
